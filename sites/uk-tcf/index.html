Beaver facts

  • Medieval church rules restricted meat during Lent, so the beaver's scaly, fish-like tail was argued to be fish — making the rest of the animal conveniently edible.
  • Castoreum, the beaver's scent-gland secretion, was prescribed for headaches and fevers and later became a perfume fixative — it contains salicylic acid from the willow they eat.
  • The Welsh afanc and the beaver's Welsh name afanc share a root, hinting at how familiar the animal once was in Britain.
  • Place names such as Beverley in Yorkshire (from Old English for "beaver lodge clearing") map roughly onto where beavers once lived.
  • Beaver-felt top hats were so fashionable in 18th-century London that a single pelt could be worth more than a labourer's weekly wage.
