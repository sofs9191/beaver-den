LFPDPPP · Mexico

El castor: Mexico's endangered engineer

At the southern edge of its range, the beaver is a protected endangered species in Mexico, holding on along a handful of desert rivers in Sonora, Chihuahua and the Río Bravo basin.

Consent test page — LFPDPPP (Ley Federal de Protección de Datos Personales en Posesión de los Particulares). Mexican template under the 2025 LFPDPPP, which replaced the 2010 law and moved oversight from INAI to the Secretaría Anticorrupción y Buen Gobierno.

Beaver facts

  • Northern Mexico is the southern edge of the North American beaver's range. In Mexico it lives only in Sonora, Chihuahua, Coahuila, Nuevo León and Tamaulipas.
  • Mexico lists the beaver as en peligro de extinción (endangered) under NOM-059-SEMARNAT-2010, even though it is rated Least Concern worldwide.
  • Dams, water extraction, grazing and trapping have split the beavers of the Río Bravo (Rio Grande) basin into small, scattered populations.
  • Sonora's beavers belong to the subspecies Castor canadensis frondator. A 1999 survey found beaver sign at 14 sites along the upper Río Bavispe, among its cottonwoods and willows.
  • Mexico's protected-areas commission, CONANP, runs a dedicated species action programme (PACE) for the beaver.
  • In Spanish the beaver is el castor, from the same Latin root as castoreum, the scent the fur trade once prized.
