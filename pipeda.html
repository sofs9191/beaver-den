The rodent on the nickel: Canada's national animal

No other country has put quite so much of its economic history on the back of a semi-aquatic rodent.

Beaver facts

  • The beaver was given official status as a symbol of Canada in 1975, and has been on the Canadian five-cent piece since 1937.
  • The North American beaver (Castor canadensis) is the second-largest rodent on Earth, after the capybara — adults typically weigh 16–30 kg.
  • The transcontinental fur trade in beaver pelts drove much of the early European mapping of Canada, and the Hudson's Bay Company was founded on it in 1670.
  • Castoreum is still occasionally used as a natural flavouring, which is why "natural vanilla flavour" has an unfortunate reputation it mostly doesn't deserve.
  • Canada's beaver population has recovered to an estimated six to twelve million, from near-collapse at the height of the fur trade.
